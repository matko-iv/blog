AI image processing on Samsung phones and its flaws (Scene Optimizer and "Refining Picture")
25 February 2026

Scene Optimizer and related options such as "Intelligent optimization" or the "Refining picture" message are part of Samsung's AI system for automatic scene recognition and photo post-processing.
Scene Optimizer uses machine learning models to recognize the type of scene (e.g. food, night, landscape, text, moon) and then changes colors, contrast, sharpness and other parameters so that the subject looks "better" without manual adjustment. On newer devices such as the Galaxy S24, this system is part of a wider ProVisual/AI engine that also includes more advanced editing features, like generative fill and AI editing suggestions.

## How it works
Scene Optimizer was first introduced with the Galaxy S10 series, and since then it has used neural networks trained on large image datasets to classify the scene the camera sees in real time. When the algorithm recognizes a matching scene type, the phone automatically applies a set of predefined adjustments: raising or lowering saturation, local contrast adjustment (local tone mapping) and noise reduction. For specific objects like the moon it also uses multi-frame superresolution: the device takes several frames, combines them, reduces noise and adds detail with an AI model trained on the different phases of the moon.

The "Refining picture" message that appears after taking a photo marks the post-processing stage, in which the captured RAW or basic JPEG goes through an AI pipeline before the final version is displayed and permanently saved. At high resolutions (50 MP, 200 MP) and with options like Scene Optimizer, Super HDR and others turned on, this step can take longer and use more RAM and temporary storage. Users have reported cases where "Refining picture" hangs for an unusually long time. This is usually treated as a bug and temporarily solved by clearing the camera's cache or restarting, until a software update.

## Where is this "phantom" data stored?
Samsung doesn't publicly document the detailed internal folder layout for all its temporary AI files, but the camera and gallery use app space in internal storage (the data partition) and system media folders for cache, thumbnails and image versions. Post-processing and AI optimization involve creating temporary files, intermediate results and extra copies of images that are later merged or deleted, similar to how multi-frame HDR and super-resolution features work on other phones. In practice these temporary and helper files sometimes don't get cleaned up properly, so memory usage rises sharply under items such as "Camera", "System" or "Other" storage.

On its official pages Samsung describes Scene Optimizer mainly through its effect on image quality, and doesn't explicitly mention limits on the space it can take up. However, forums and user communities record cases where the camera app and related AI features consume tens of gigabytes, which shows up as almost full internal memory with no clear explanation in the standard storage UI. In some reported scenarios clearing the cache for the Camera and Gallery apps helps, while more extreme cases end in a factory reset of the device (as happened to me) when the system storage is already seriously fragmented or blocked.

## How AI processes images on the Galaxy S24 series
With the Galaxy S24 series, Samsung promotes the ProVisual Engine, a set of AI modules that work from the moment of framing to later editing in the gallery. During capture it uses multi-frame techniques, gyro data analysis, dedicated ISP blocks and deep learning models for noise reduction and detail enhancement, especially in night scenes or at high zoom. After capture, additional AI features such as Edit Suggestions and Generative Edit can create new pixels (generative content), fix the framing or fill in the background, with the EXIF/metadata and a visual watermark marking that generative AI was used.

Samsung claims that most of the AI image processing, scene recognition and optimization runs locally on the device, inside a system protected by the Knox platform and additional security mechanisms. For newer Galaxy AI features, users have the option of limiting or disabling online data processing in the Advanced Intelligence settings, which reduces the sending of images or their representations to servers for processing. Still, the very fact that the system automatically creates extra copies and generates content means **that in the case of full memory, a software bug or improper cache cleanup, data loss, file corruption or forced resets can occur, which is a direct security risk for user data.**

So to recap, the main practical risk of AI image optimization on Samsung devices isn't classic data theft, but unpredictable consumption of memory and resources that the user can't clearly see through the interface. If the temporary files and cache of AI processing grow uncontrollably, the system can run out of the free space it needs for logs, updates, system processes and normal app operation, which increases the chance of app crashes, failed data writes and, in the worst case, data corruption, both personal and system! To reduce the risk: turn off Scene Optimizer or Intelligent optimization when taking photos in bulk, clear the camera and gallery cache from time to time, use Pro/RAW modes for critical shots and back up regularly. That solves the problem, but this procedure shouldn't be necessary at all for us to feel sure our data is protected.
