Why weather forecasts for Montenegro are so bad
8 February 2026

If you live in Budva and have ever looked at a weather forecast, you've probably thought: "Do these people even know where Montenegro is?" The answer is barely.

Montenegro is literally the worst possible scenario for weather forecasting. Imagine you're building a model: you have a nice 25x25 km grid, and each cell has one temperature value. Now, there are also gradients, so the resolution technically gets improved, to cells of 20° each. That can be done over wider areas without such a "complex" topography.

And then you get to Montenegro.

In a single 25 km cell you have:
- **The sea** at 0 m (warm, humid, calm)
- **Lovćen** at 1749 m (cold, windy, cloudy)
- **Lake Skadar** (a huge body of water with its own microclimate)
- etc

The model says: "The temperature for this cell is 12°C." It's 18°C in Budva, 6°C in Cetinje and -2°C on Lovćen. Well done, you've guessed nothing!


My favorite examples from the data:

- **ECMWF** (supposedly the best model in the world) systematically lowers the temperature by a couple of degrees throughout the year. Why? Because to it, Budva = the mountain behind it, partly "flattened". It literally flattened Lovćen.
- **GFS** (the American model) once forecast 45 km/h winds for Budva, while the sea was completely calm. It probably meant Lovćen. Or Chicago...

The solution is actually banal when you think about it. If you know ECMWF overdoes the temperature *every time*, you just correct it. If GFS *always* gives too much wind in summer, you lower it. And so on for every possible scenario. That's where the model gets trained, on these specific weather phenomena.

## What's actually needed

Ideally someone would build a **regional model with <2km resolution** specifically for Montenegro. A model that knows Budva isn't Lovćen and that the Bay of Kotor has its own microclimate.

But that costs money, and someone has to build and maintain it.

Until then, you have [my correction](https://matko-iv.github.io/vrijeme/forecast.html). It isn't perfect, but it's better than the original.
