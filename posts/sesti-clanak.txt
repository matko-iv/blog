A serious security risk for your data on Samsung phones
25 February 2026

I recently had a serious problem with a Samsung S24+ that cost me a factory reset and hours of restoring data, so I decided to share the experience.

I took a large number of photos for a project — I needed them to construct a 3D scene of what was being photographed, which meant hundreds of photos in a short time (over 150). The Samsung S24+, like all newer phones with UI7+, has a feature called "Refining Picture", an AI optimization that processes every photo after it's taken. Starting from UI 7.0 it's automatically turned on at maximum strength.

After a while the phone told me the memory was full. When I checked, the camera app (so not the Gallery, where the photos are, but the "Camera" app) was taking up about 90GB of space. All of the phone's remaining memory was taken up by temporary files the AI generated while processing the photos. The system practically couldn't write anything, apps were crashing, and I still didn't know what was going on.

I tried clearing the cache, first from the UI, then I booted the phone into Recovery mode to wipe the whole cache partition, then I connected it to a computer with Android Build Tools and tried to find those 90GB, but they weren't in the main data folder, so the only place those gigabytes could be was `root`, and playing around with root could only make things worse.

This isn't just my problem. On the Samsung community and Reddit there are loads of users with the S23 Ultra, S24 Ultra and S25 Ultra reporting the same thing. Photos stuck in the "Refining" state for 15+ hours, a camera app eating up storage without any warning.

Worst of all, there's no limit whatsoever. The app can take as much space as it wants and the system doesn't warn you in time. That's a security risk, because full memory can also lead to data corruption, not just a slow phone. Luckily, my phone still managed to recognize the connected computer, so I could back up my data right away. But for someone else it might not have done that.

If you're taking a lot of photos at once, turn off Scene Optimizer and Refining Picture in the camera settings before you start. Or use Pro Mode, which shoots RAW without any AI processing. I learned this the hard way.

A serious failure by Samsung. AI could have compromised my data.
