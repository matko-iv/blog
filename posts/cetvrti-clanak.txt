A guide to the web infrastructure of Montenegro's government bodies
4 February 2026

**Warning**: This article contains potentially traumatic descriptions of government websites. If you want to stay sane, maybe don't read any further...

## Welcome to the digital scream

Montenegro is a small country full of corruption and cocaine with ambitions to join the EU. We have beautiful nature, fantastic food, and web infrastructure that looks like it was built by someone with several kinds of drugs in their bloodstream.

Let's take a walk through the digital landscape of our beloved country.

## Gov.me — the portal that sometimes works

The central portal [gov.me](https://www.gov.me) is, to be fair, visually acceptable. It even works. Most of the time. Except when it doesn't. Which is usually exactly when you need it.

But try finding something specific. Say, information on how to register a vehicle. You get 47 categories, 12 subcategories and 6 links leading to the same PDF from 2019.

Digitalization: the famous process of turning paperwork into PDF paperwork.

## eUprava — a brave attempt

[eUprava](https://euprava.gov.me) is a system meant to let you handle all your government business online. And indeed, some things you *can* handle online. For example, you can check whether your ID card has expired. And then go to the counter to renew it.

The system requires a digital proof of identity, which requires a special card reader, which requires a driver that only works on Windows 7, which requires... that you give up and go to the counter.

My favorite detail: a number of government sites went years without HTTPS. In 2024. So when you accessed a site holding your personal data, the connection wasn't encrypted. **E viva!!!!** For those who don't know what that means: imagine sending a letter with your personal data, but instead of an envelope you use a postcard. And the postman reads it. And the neighbor reads it. And everyone reads it.

At least they had an animation on the loading screen. Awesome!!

## The website of the municipality of [insert any municipality]

Every Montenegrin municipality has its own website. And every one of those sites looks like it was made by somebody's nephew who "knows computers".

## "As long as he's alive and well!"

Some rules that apply to almost all of them:

1. **The front page news is from 2022.** — but the site technically works, so... success?
2. **The contact page** has a phone that doesn't ring and an email nobody reads
3. **PDFs** are scanned papers, skewed at a 7° angle, at a resolution that barely shows the text. But they have a stamp! A scanned stamp counts

## Why this matters

Jokes aside, this actually *is* a problem. People lose time, work slows down, tourists get confused, investors give up. Good web infrastructure is the foundation of a functioning state, not a luxury. Montenegro has great IT professionals who can build excellent sites and apps. **Hire people who know what they're doing**, we do not recommend nepotismmaxxing.

But realistically, if you're reading this, you're probably not the person who decides. So let's just have a laugh and keep using the counters.
