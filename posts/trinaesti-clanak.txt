Project Milosevic: First Screenshots of a PSX-Style Belgrade
25. jul 2026

One walkable block of central Belgrade runs at 320×180, textured low, with a first-person controller and collision that holds. I am posting now because the look is settled. Everything after this is research, and the research runs through the rest of 2026.

![The Kralja Milana corridor in the running Godot build, at the actual 320×180 internal resolution](posts/13,1.png)

## What it is meant to be

A detective game set in Belgrade in March and April 1999, during the NATO bombing of Yugoslavia. You play Andrej, an inspector who loses his job in the second week of the campaign and keeps working the case without a badge, in a city under blackout.

I wrote one test scene to see whether the idea survives contact with a page. Two men in a chief's office: taped windows, no electricity, a closed folder on the desk that turns out to hold Andrej's own file. Neither of them says the real thing out loud. A siren starts outside at the end, and neither man looks up.

That scene sets the rule for the environment. It has to carry investigation and uncomfortable waiting, and it must never turn the bombing into an action set piece.

## The test area

I traced one polygon over a satellite view and threw away everything outside it: Slavija, Manjež, the Kralja Milana and Nemanjina corridor, the edge of the General Staff complex.

![The traced test polygon. Everything outside the red line was deleted from the build](posts/13,2.png)

That comes to 0.29 km². Small enough that I can finish it, dense enough to read as a real place. Slavija gives me an open square, Manjež a park with sightlines, Nemanjina a long street with a tram line down the middle.

The geometry starts as present-day OpenStreetMap. Blender imports it, crops it to the polygon, textures it, and exports one scene to Godot.

## Why it looks like this

The game renders into a 320×180 viewport and scales it up by whole numbers. Textures use nearest filtering, lighting is per-vertex, and depth fog closes off the far end of every street. The vertex shader snaps positions onto the low-resolution grid, which brings back the wobble PlayStation hardware had.

Nostalgia is part of it. The rest is honesty about sourcing. A photographic 1999 Belgrade would need documented detail I cannot find for every facade on Kralja Milana, and it would ask you to read the environment as a reconstruction. At 320×180 the city reads as a drawing of a place, which is what it is.

## What is fake on purpose

Two things in these screenshots are placeholders. I would rather name them than let them slide past.

![The СЛАВИЈА rooftop letters and the banner beneath them](posts/13,6.png)

The rooftop lettering and the banner come from a reference image with no date on it. I am not presenting the building choice or the wording as an authenticated April 1999 fact.

![ДУНАВ ОСИГУРАЊЕ in the running build](posts/13,7.png)

The storefronts have the same problem. My photograph carries a 1993 date. It gives me a plausible period vocabulary and it establishes no address in 1999. Every shopfront carries an "unverified" tag in the build report.

OpenStreetMap describes the city of 2026. The build still holds hotels that were not there in 1999, a bank finished in 2006, and the present-day ruins of the General Staff. The ruins are the sharp problem. I need that building intact in March 1999 and damaged later, and twenty-seven years of weather on a ruin gives me neither state.

So I treat the raw import as a survey of the ground I am building on rather than as a historical authority. The pipeline stays split, which lets me correct the city without regenerating it.

## What comes next

Dated variants instead of permanent edits. The intact city first, then early April with the blackout and the taped windows, then single locations where the script actually goes.

Before the end of the year I want four things: a controller built for examining things rather than for proving collision works, a readable UI over a viewport this small, period vehicles and props as reusable kits, and the office scene playable start to finish. Interiors are the biggest hole. Nothing here has a door you can open.

No release date. Generating this blockout was fast. Researching the same block will not be. Expect more updates through the rest of 2026, including the ones where something did not work.

## One constraint that will not move

Real victims do not become collectibles, set dressing, or shock imagery. The game ends on the night of 23 April at the RTS building, and it cuts before the strike. Sixteen people died there. The text does not name them and does not use them.

Andrej is there because he came for a document that would clear him, and that is why he dies. The scandal killed him and the bomb killed him, and the game never separates which came first. Any detail that makes that harder to feel comes out.
